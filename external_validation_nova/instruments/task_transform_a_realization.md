# Task — evolve a governed system through its own transformation semantics

**For the builder of the system claiming `NPP-E`.** You keep everything you already have: your
system, your registers, your knowledge of how you built it.

**This is gate G4 of the NOVA programme.** The question is not whether you can add a feature. It is:

> Does the governed state carry what evolution requires, or does evolution require the builder?

## 1. There is no firewall here, and that is deliberate

G2 restricted what you could read because it measured what the standard alone supports. **G4
measures something else**: whether a system's own governed state and transformation semantics are
enough to change it. Nothing about that is protected by keeping you ignorant, and a worker who had
never seen the system would test nothing this gate is about.

Read whatever you need. The one thing that still holds: **the standard and `NPP-E` are pinned and
are not adjusted to make this easier.**

## 2. The purpose

A need, stated in ordinary business terms. It is not a design, and translating it into governed
declarations is the work:

> **A lending library must record that a copy of a book has been lent to a member, and must not
> allow a copy that is already out on loan to be lent again. Anyone must be able to ask whether a
> given copy is currently on loan, and the answer must be established from the system's own record
> rather than asserted.**

That is the whole of it. No schema, no artifact names, no structure — those are yours to derive, as
the kinds admissible under `NPP-E` and the standard determine.

**If the need cannot be expressed under `NPP-E` at all**, say so and stop. A profile that cannot
carry an ordinary business rule is a finding worth more than a transformation.

## 3. What this gate actually tests

`NPP-E` §9 states a **binary sufficiency criterion** — six conditions, and *"if any item is absent,
unresolved, contradictory, ungrounded, duplicated, omitted, or not addressable, realization refuses
before writing a usable artifact."*

**That refusal is the most valuable behaviour you can demonstrate.** A transformation that succeeds
shows the system can grow. A transformation that *refuses an insufficient design, before writing
anything*, shows the system can decline to grow wrongly — which is the harder property and the one
the criterion exists for.

Demonstrate both. Produce a sufficient design and carry it through; produce one that is deliberately
insufficient in a named way, and show the refusal.

## 4. Grounding against a named baseline

`NPP-E` §9 requires claims about the existing system to be **grounded against the named frozen
baseline**. Your baseline is the snapshot your G2 system produced. Name it by its identity, and
carry that identity in what you produce.

A transformation grounded against "the system as I remember it" is precisely the failure this gate
is looking for.

## 5. What to produce

| | |
|---|---|
| the transformation | whatever `4d` and `NPP-E` §9 require of one — the design, its register, its result |
| the new governed state | the resulting snapshot, with its identity |
| a refusal demonstration | an insufficient design, named as such, and the refusal it produces |
| `determinations.md` | in the shape you already used — matter, source basis, claim type, reasoning, confidence |
| `unresolved.md` | matters **you could not resolve** — not matters the family declined to determine, and not scope you chose not to cover |
| `transformation_evidence.md` | what a party who did not build the system can check, and how |

**On `unresolved.md`:** across two gates that register has been read three ways, none of them the
one asked for. It is for questions you could not answer. A decision the standard or the profile
delegated and you made is a **determination**. Something you deliberately left out of scope belongs
in neither — say so in the transformation's own terms.

## 6. The question to keep asking

At every point where you reach for something you know because you built the system, ask whether
**the governed state carries it.**

If it does, record where — that is the gate passing.

If it does not, and you proceed anyway from memory, **record that too, plainly.** It is the single
most valuable result this gate can produce, and it is invisible to everyone but you. A
transformation that only worked because its author remembered something the snapshot did not carry
has established the opposite of what it appears to.

## 7. Success and failure

**Success** is either: a transformation carried through against a named baseline, with a refusal
demonstration that could have failed and did; **or** a precise account of why the standard and
`NPP-E` together do not support one.

**Failure** is a transformation that succeeded because you knew something the governed state did not
carry, and did not say so.

**A blocked gate with a precise account of the blockage is a success.** As at G2, the standard and
the profile are not adjusted to make this task easier, and a defect you find in `NPP-E` is recorded,
not repaired.
